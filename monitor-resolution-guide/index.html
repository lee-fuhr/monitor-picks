Monitor resolution guide

Updated June 2026Independently researchedNo paid placement.

Quick answer

The right resolution for you depends on your screen size, viewing distance, primary use, and graphics hardware. For most people, 1440p offers the best balance of sharpness and performance, while 4K excels for detailed creative work and 1080p remains a solid budget or high-refresh-rate gaming pick.

What do 1080p, 1440p, and 4K actually mean?

These numbers refer to the horizontal pixel count in a display: 1080p has 1,920 horizontal pixels (Full HD), 1440p has 2,560 (often called QHD or WQHD), and 4K has 3,840 (Ultra HD). The resolution determines how much detail the screen can show at once. More pixels mean sharper text and finer image detail, but they also require more processing power to drive, especially in games. The same resolution can look very different on a small monitor versus a large one because pixel density changes.

How does screen size affect the best resolution?

Pixel density (measured in pixels per inch, or PPI) is the real factor behind sharpness. On a 24-inch monitor, 1080p looks reasonably crisp to most people sitting at a normal distance. On a 27-inch panel, 1080p starts to look grainy, while 1440p hits a sweet spot. At 32 inches and above, 4K is the standard to avoid visible pixels. If you sit unusually close to a large monitor, you may benefit from a higher resolution even at a moderate size. A good rule of thumb: aim for roughly 90–110 PPI for comfortable everyday use without scaling.

What is scaling and why does it matter for 4K?

Scaling is how your operating system enlarges text, icons, and UI elements so they aren’t tiny on a high-resolution display. On a 4K monitor at native resolution, everything appears very small unless you sit very close. Windows and macOS both offer scaling options (e.g., 150% or 200%). Scaling works well on modern operating systems, but some older or niche apps can look blurry or misaligned. macOS handles scaling more seamlessly with its Retina approach, while Windows may occasionally require logging out and back in. If you mainly use apps that don’t scale well, 1440p can be a simpler choice.

Which resolution is best for gaming?

It depends on your graphics card and the frame rate you want. 1080p is the easiest to drive, so it’s still the top choice for high-refresh-rate competitive gaming (like 144 Hz or 240 Hz) on mid-range GPUs. 1440p offers a significant visual upgrade and is now the “sweet spot” for many gamers, especially with GPUs like an RTX 4060 or better. 4K gaming demands a very powerful GPU, even with upscaling technologies like DLSS or FSR. If you play slower, single-player titles and value image quality over frame rate, 4K is rewarding. For fast-paced esports, 1080p or 1440p with high refresh rates is usually the better fit.

Which resolution is best for productivity and multitasking?

More pixels let you fit more windows side by side. 1440p on a 27-inch screen gives you comfortable screen real estate for coding, writing, and browsing without scaling. 4K on a 32-inch monitor feels like two virtual 1080p panels, which is excellent for spreadsheets, video editing timelines, or referencing multiple documents. Be aware that at 4K on a 27-inch screen, you’ll likely need scaling (e.g., 150%) to read text comfortably, which slightly reduces the usable area. For pure productivity without scaling, 1440p at 27 inches or 4K at 32 inches (or larger) is a common recommendation.

Which resolution is best for photo and video editing?

4K is the standard for professional video editing because many timelines and previews fit without constant zooming. Photo editors also benefit from seeing more detail in a raw file at 100% zoom. 4K panels often come with better color accuracy and wider gamut, but that’s not guaranteed by resolution alone, look for IPS or OLED and good factory calibration. 1440p can still be productive for editing, especially if your graphics card doesn’t drive a 4K monitor smoothly or if you prefer a higher refresh rate. 1080p is less ideal for critical detail work but can work for basic edits on a budget.

Frequently asked questions

Can I run a 1440p signal on a 1080p monitor?

No, a monitor can only display its native resolution clearly. Sending a 1440p signal to a 1080p monitor will result in downscaling, which often looks softer than native 1080p. Use the monitor’s native resolution for the best image.

Is 4K worth it for a 27-inch monitor?

Yes, if you value extreme sharpness and have the GPU power, but expect to use scaling (typically 150% on Windows or 200% on macOS). Without scaling, text will be too small for most people. For gaming, 4K at 27 inches demands a top-tier GPU.

Does 1440p look blurry on a 24-inch monitor?

Not at all, 1440p on a 24-inch screen gives a very high pixel density (~122 PPI), which makes text and images extremely sharp. The downside is that Windows scaling may be needed to make text readable, and some users prefer the more natural PPI of 1080p at that size.

What GPU do I need for 1440p gaming?

A mid-range modern GPU like an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3060 Ti or AMD Radeon RX 6700 XT can handle 1440p at high settings in many games at 60+ fps. For higher refresh rates (120 Hz or more), you’ll want something like an RTX 4070 or RX 7800 XT.

Is 1080p still good in 2025?

Absolutely. 1080p remains the most common resolution for budget monitors and high-refresh-rate esports gaming. It requires less GPU power, so you can save money or invest in a faster refresh rate. It’s also fine for general office work and web browsing on smaller screens.

How does macOS handle 4K scaling compared to Windows?

macOS uses a Retina approach where the UI is rendered at double the resolution than the display, then scaled down, usually at 200% on 4K. This gives crisp text. Windows offers fractional scaling (125%, 150%, 175%) which can cause slight blurring in some older apps, but modern Windows works well.

In shortDon’t treat resolution as an absolute ranking, pair it with the right screen size and consider your hardware and typical tasks. 1440p is the most flexible all-rounder today, 4K rewards you in creative work and immersive gaming if you have the GPU, and 1080p still makes sense for tight budgets or competitive play. If you can, see a monitor in person before buying to judge sharpness and scaling for yourself.
