Monitor refresh rate explained

Updated June 2026Independently researchedNo paid placement.

Quick answer

A monitor’s refresh rate determines how many times per second the screen updates, with 60Hz being fine for basic tasks and casual gaming, 144Hz offering a noticeable smoothness improvement that most gamers will appreciate, and 240Hz providing marginal gains best reserved for competitive esports players who can maintain very high frame rates.

What does a monitor’s refresh rate actually mean?

Refresh rate, measured in hertz (Hz), tells you how many times your monitor redraws the image each second. A 60Hz screen updates 60 times per second, while a 144Hz screen updates 144 times. In practice, that means motion appears smoother and more fluid the higher you go, especially during fast-paced content like games or scrolling. It’s important to separate refresh rate from frame rate: the monitor’s refresh rate is the hardware limit on how often it can show a new image, while your GPU’s frame rate is how many frames it can produce. For the smoothest experience you want your frame rate to match or exceed your monitor’s refresh rate, otherwise you’re leaving smoothness on the table.

Does 60Hz still make sense in 2025?

Absolutely, but only for the right use case. A 60Hz monitor is still perfectly fine for office work, browsing, watching videos, and casual gaming in slower-paced genres like turn-based strategy or narrative adventures. They’re also the most budget-friendly option, often costing a couple hundred dollars less than high-refresh alternatives. The trade-off becomes clear the moment you play a fast game like a first-person shooter or racing title. Motion will feel choppier, and quick camera movements can cause noticeable blur. Even general desktop use can feel a little sluggish once you’ve experienced anything above 60Hz. So if you mostly do everyday tasks and don’t play competitive or action-heavy games, 60Hz is still a very sensible pick.

What does 144Hz feel like and who should buy it?

Jumping from 60Hz to 144Hz is the single most noticeable upgrade you can make for gaming. Motion becomes dramatically smoother, mouse movements feel more immediate, and fast-paced scenes lose that stuttery quality. Many owners describe it as making 60Hz suddenly feel “laggy” in comparison. Even scrolling through a web page or moving windows around feels eerily fluid. A 144Hz monitor is the sweet spot for most gamers. It doesn’t require a top-tier graphics card: a mid-range GPU can easily push 100–144 frames per second in many titles at reasonable settings. This refresh rate is ideal for anyone who plays a mix of competitive shooters, action RPGs, or racing games and wants a clear improvement without the heavy cost or hardware demands of higher rates.

Is 240Hz worth the jump, or is it just for esports?

240Hz is a true diminishing-returns upgrade. The difference between 144Hz and 240Hz is far subtler than the leap from 60Hz to 144Hz. You’ll notice slightly less motion blur and a tiny bit more responsiveness, but most people need to be actively looking for it. For everyday use or single-player games, the advantage is negligible. Where 240Hz shines is in competitive esports, titles like Valorant, Counter-Strike, Apex Legends, or Overwatch where every millisecond of reduced latency can give you an edge. Professional or highly skilled players will appreciate the smoother tracking and faster reaction times. But you also need a powerful GPU to consistently hit 240 frames per second in those games, and that can be expensive. If you aren’t playing esports at a high level, you’ll likely get more value from a high-quality 144Hz panel with better colors or resolution.

How does your GPU affect which refresh rate you should choose?

Your graphics card is the gatekeeper. A monitor can only show as many frames as your GPU can deliver. If you buy a 144Hz monitor but your GPU only manages 60 fps in your favorite games, you’ll get no benefit from the higher refresh rate, you’ll just be paying for something you can’t use. So before upgrading, check the frame rates your GPU can achieve in the games you play most. For a 144Hz target, a modern mid-range graphics card paired with a 1080p or 1440p monitor is a realistic combination. For 240Hz, you generally need a high-end card, especially at resolutions above 1080p. If you’re on a tight GPU budget, a 60Hz or perhaps a 75Hz monitor might be a smarter choice, and you can upgrade the monitor later when you upgrade the GPU. Adaptive sync technologies like G-Sync and FreeSync help by matching the monitor’s refresh rate to the GPU’s output, which minimizes tearing and stuttering when frame rates fluctuate.

When is a higher refresh rate simply wasted?

Higher refresh rates are wasted whenever your content or hardware can’t take advantage of them. Watching standard 24 or 30 fps movies on a 240Hz monitor doesn’t make them look smoother, they’ll just be displayed as-is with possible judder if the refresh rate isn’t a clean multiple of the source. Similarly, if you mostly use your computer for email, spreadsheets, and YouTube, 144Hz or 240Hz offers no practical benefit over a good 60Hz panel. Console gaming also caps out: most current consoles are limited to 60 to 120Hz, so a 240Hz monitor is overkill unless you also play PC games at high frame rates. And if your GPU can’t consistently push above 60 fps in the titles you play, even a 144Hz monitor won’t feel any smoother than a 60Hz one. In those cases, your money is better spent on a higher-resolution or better-color monitor rather than chasing hertz you won’t use.

Frequently asked questions

Can the human eye actually see the difference between 60Hz and 144Hz?

Yes, most people can clearly perceive the difference in smoothness and reduced motion blur, especially during fast movement. The idea that the eye only sees 30 or 60 fps is a myth, our visual system processes continuous motion, and higher refresh rates provide noticeably less stutter and better clarity.

Do I need a high refresh rate monitor for console gaming?

The PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S support up to 120Hz, so a 120Hz or 144Hz monitor is beneficial for those consoles in titles that run above 60 fps. A 240Hz monitor is wasted on consoles because they cannot exceed 120Hz output.

Is there a noticeable difference between 144Hz and 240Hz?

The difference exists but is subtle compared to the jump from 60 to 144Hz. Most casual gamers will barely notice it, while competitive esports players often report slightly smoother tracking and lower perceived latency. It’s a classic case of diminishing returns.

Does refresh rate affect everyday use like browsing or office work?

Yes, but to a lesser degree. Scrolling through web pages and moving windows around feels noticeably smoother at 120Hz or higher compared to 60Hz. Many people find this makes their computer feel faster and more responsive, though it’s not essential for productivity.

Do I need G-Sync or FreeSync if I have a high refresh rate monitor?

While not strictly required, adaptive sync technologies strongly reduce screen tearing and stuttering when your frame rate fluctuates below the monitor’s refresh rate. They make the experience smoother and are highly recommended for any high-refresh gaming monitor.

What refresh rate should I get for a budget gaming build?

A 75Hz or 120Hz monitor can be a good middle ground if you have a very tight budget and a low-power GPU. However, a 144Hz monitor is often the best value if you can stretch your budget slightly, as it offers a major upgrade in smoothness without requiring a top-tier graphics card.

In shortChoosing the right refresh rate comes down to matching your hardware, the types of games you play, and your budget. 60Hz covers the basics and saves you money, 144Hz is the community-recommended sweet spot for most gamers, and 240Hz is a niche tool for competitive esports players with the GPU to back it up. Don’t chase numbers you won’t actually use, a well-balanced setup that pairs your monitor with a capable graphics card will always deliver a better experience than raw Hertz alone.
